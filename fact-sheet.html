Fact sheet

Kansas SB 30: occupational licensing and the Legislature

Prepared by the Kansas Beauty and Barber Professionals Consumer Advocates (BBPCA).

What SB 30 does

  • It requires covered state agencies to submit annual occupational-license reports to the Joint Committee on Administrative Rules and Regulations.[1]
  • It requires legislative ratification for new occupational licenses and material changes to existing occupational licenses adopted by a covered state agency.[1]

The Governor’s veto

On April 6, 2026, Governor Laura Kelly vetoed SB 30. In her message, she called it “another inappropriate power grab,” said legislators lack the technical knowledge to judge these decisions, and said it would add “bureaucratic red tape and bloated administrative busy work.”[2]

The Legislature later overrode the veto.[3]

Why BBPCA is concerned

These are BBPCA’s policy concerns, not a neutral summary of the bill:

  • SB 30 adds a legislative ratification step for covered agencies. Kansas agencies also participate in a budget process that includes strategic plans, goals, and performance measures, and state regulations are subject to existing review requirements. BBPCA is concerned that additional approval steps could delay needed updates and make the process more cumbersome.[4][5]
  • Occupational licensing requirements are grounded in profession-specific statutes, while SB 30 establishes a broad process for covered agencies and lists exceptions. BBPCA is concerned that a general process may affect professions differently and add complexity for agencies administering distinct licensing laws.[1]
  • BBPCA believes decisions about training, safety, and consumer protection should continue to draw on the expertise of the agencies responsible for those areas.
  • BBPCA supports independent oversight and meaningful representation of licensed professionals and consumers in decisions affecting their professions.

What BBPCA is asking for

From the Legislature (changes to law)

  • Repeal SB 30.
  • Require clear disclosure to consumers when a service is not regulated or licensed by the state.
  • Provide fair and meaningful representation of licensed professionals on the Kansas Board of Cosmetology. This includes giving body art and tanning their own boards, and ensuring the number of board representatives for each profession is fair in proportion to the number of licensees in that profession.

From the Kansas Board of Cosmetology and KDHE (regulations)

  • Reasonable licensure standards and oversight that protect consumers, uphold professional qualifications, and maintain public trust.

A commitment from legislators on occupational licensing bills

Lawmakers should seek input from licensed professionals, educators, business owners, and consumers when considering changes to the laws that govern licensed industries. BBPCA asks every legislator to:

  • Actively seek both sides of the story, and give small agency leaders and practitioners as much time as lobbyists and cabinet-level officials, especially when a bill affects the industry that agency governs.
  • Educate themselves on the issues and laws they are voting on, and read and understand each bill before voting.
  • Not vote to approve occupational licensing bills unless they have actively pursued input from the industry professionals, educators, and consumers in the areas they represent.

Check a license

Consumers can confirm a practitioner or establishment is licensed with the Kansas Board of Cosmetology license lookup.
