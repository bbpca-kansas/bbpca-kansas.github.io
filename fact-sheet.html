Fact sheet

Kansas SB 30: occupational licensing and the Legislature

Prepared by the Kansas Beauty and Barber Professionals Consumer Advocates (BBPCA).

What SB 30 does

  • It requires state agencies to send occupational licensing annual reports to the Joint Committee on Administrative Rules and Regulations.
  • It requires the Legislature to approve, by ratification, the adoption of new occupational licenses and material changes to existing ones.

Source: Governor’s veto message for SB 30 (the bill’s official title and summary).

The Governor’s veto

On April 6, 2026, Governor Laura Kelly vetoed SB 30. In her message she called it “another inappropriate power grab,” said legislators lack the technical knowledge to judge these decisions, and said it would add “bureaucratic red tape and bloated administrative busy work.”

The Legislature later overrode the veto. See the Kansas Legislature’s SB 30 page for the bill’s full history and votes.

Why BBPCA is concerned

  • New occupational licenses and material changes to existing ones now need legislative approval, which adds steps and delay. Agencies already report extensively, including their goals and outcomes, through the budget approval process with the Kansas Division of the Budget and the Legislative Division of Budget in both the Senate and the House, and they must already take all regulation updates through JCARR (the Joint Committee on Administrative Rules and Regulations). SB 30 adds another element to an already cumbersome system, which can result in outdated laws and regulations and complex policies and procedures.
  • Every profession under a licensing board is governed by different statutes that authorize different regulations. SB 30 is a general model bill that applies to all of them, so it affects some agencies differently than others. BBPCA is concerned this creates confusion and cumbersome application procedures and requirements, because agencies must navigate acts outside their own professional licensing acts.
  • Decisions about training, safety, and consumer protection may move away from agencies with technical expertise.
  • Licensed professionals pay for their regulatory boards through their own license fees, and the boards should remain independent, as intended, to balance the powers of government.

What BBPCA is asking for

From the Legislature (changes to law)

  • Repeal SB 30.
  • Require clear disclosure to consumers when a service is not regulated or licensed by the state.
  • Provide fair and meaningful representation of licensed professionals on the Kansas Board of Cosmetology. This includes giving body art and tanning their own boards, and ensuring the number of board representatives for each profession is fair in proportion to the number of licensees in that profession.

From the Kansas Board of Cosmetology and KDHE (regulations)

  • Reasonable licensure standards and oversight that protect consumers, uphold professional qualifications, and maintain public trust.

A commitment from legislators on occupational licensing bills

Lawmakers should seek input from licensed professionals, educators, business owners, and consumers when considering changes to the laws that govern licensed industries. BBPCA asks every legislator to:

  • Actively seek both sides of the story, and give small agency leaders and practitioners as much time as lobbyists and cabinet-level officials, especially when a bill affects the industry that agency governs.
  • Educate themselves on the issues and laws they are voting on, and read and understand each bill before voting.
  • Not vote to approve occupational licensing bills unless they have actively pursued input from the industry professionals, educators, and consumers in the areas they represent.

Check a license

Consumers can confirm a practitioner or establishment is licensed with the Kansas Board of Cosmetology license lookup.
