01 / Services

Exemptions and proposals

Exemption from the definition of cosmetology is not the same as a statement that every related practice or facility is unregulated. Read the conditions in the law.

In law · July 1, 2000

Twisting, wrapping, weaving, extending, locking and braiding

Kansas excluded these tension-based hair services from cosmetology when they meet statutory conditions, including restrictions on dyes and chemicals that alter hair structure. The exemption requires the infection-control brochure and completed self-test to be available at the service location.

Sources: K.S.A. 65-1901 · K.S.A. 65-1928

In law · July 1, 2022

Threading

SB 348 exempted the defined practice of threading from cosmetology. The law limits the method and areas of service and requires the infection-control brochure and completed self-test at the location. Licensed cosmetologists and estheticians may also perform threading.

Sources: Legislative summary of SB 348 · K.S.A. 65-1901 · K.S.A. 65-1928

Veto sustained · 2024

Sugaring: SB 434

SB 434 proposed exempting hair removal by sugaring from cosmetology. Governor Laura Kelly returned the bill with a veto on April 12, 2024; the Senate made no motion to reconsider and the veto was sustained on April 30. The proposed exemption did not become law. Litigation is separate from the bill and may affect the issue.

Sources: SB 434 legislative history · Current definition of cosmetology

02 / Occupational licensing

Senate Bill 30

In law · May 7, 2026

Legislative review of licensing

SB 30 requires covered agencies to submit annual occupational licensing reports and requires legislative ratification before agency-proposed new occupational licenses or material changes to existing licenses take effect. The act excludes certain health licensing boards and applies to proposals made on or after January 1, 2026.

Governor Kelly vetoed the bill on April 6; both chambers overrode the veto on April 9. The act took effect upon publication in the Kansas Register on May 7, 2026.

Sources: 2026 Session Laws, chapter 145 · SB 30 bill history · BBPCA fact sheet

BBPCA advocacy position

Why we are watching

BBPCA supports repeal of SB 30 and is concerned that the additional approval process could delay safety updates and reduce the role of profession-specific expertise. This is an advocacy position, not a claim that the law itself repealed existing licenses.

03 / Board oversight

Chronological list of merger attempts

These proposals differ: shared administration is not the same as merging the boards themselves.

2010 · HB 2722: shared administration

HB 2722 proposed consolidating the boards’ offices, equipment, supplies and staff and creating a jointly appointed executive director. Its text retained the two boards; it did not itself create a single regulatory board.

Source: 2010 HB 2722 as introduced · Board budget review

2017–2018 · HB 2296 and budget proposals

Following Governor Sam Brownback’s budget proposal to consolidate agencies, HB 2296 proposed a combined Kansas Board of Barbering, Cosmetology and Body Art. The bill died in the House Health and Human Services Committee in 2018. Merger language was also considered in budget legislation, including House Substitute for SB 109; the boards remained separate.

Sources: 2017–2018 HB 2296 history and text · Fiscal note · SB 109 budget explanation

2026 · RS 3343: draft opportunity act

RS 3343, the proposed Kansas Barbering and Cosmetology Opportunity Act, was presented in the Senate Federal and State Affairs Committee in February 2026. Reporting describes a proposed nine-member combined board, combined fee funds and a supervised-practice licensing pathway. It was a draft, not an enacted law; a numbered bill and final disposition have not been independently confirmed here.

Source: Citizen Portal committee report (AI-generated summary; consult the meeting record for full context).

04 / Adult care settings

KDADS and facility exemptions

In law · July 1, 2023

HB 2125 and adult care facilities

HB 2125 amended K.S.A. 65-1904a to exempt adult care homes and long-term care units of medical care facilities from that section’s salon or clinic provisions, including facility licensing and inspection. This is a facility exemption, not a blanket exemption from all laws or individual practitioner licensing.

HB 2408 addressed a related proposal, with KDADS testimony, but died in Senate committee. The enacted measure was HB 2125, chapter 40 of the 2023 Session Laws.

Sources: 2023 Session Laws, chapter 40 · K.S.A. 65-1904a · HB 2408 history and testimony

05 / A voice at the table

Who represents the professions?

The Kansas Board of Cosmetology’s responsibilities and membership have changed over time. BBPCA advocates meaningful representation for licensed professionals and consumers.

Historical composition

Before expansion: five voting members

The earlier governor-appointed board had two licensed shop operators who were also registered cosmetologists, two registered cosmetologists, and one public member. The statute provided for a member from each congressional district and the remainder at large, with no more than three from one political party.

In 1992, tanning oversight was added without a tanning voting seat. The 1996 body art law added a nonvoting advisory member, effective in 1997.

Sources: 1996 Session Laws, chapter 138 · Statutory history

Current statutory composition

Eight seats across professions and the public

Changes in 1998 and 2002 helped establish the current structure:

  • Three cosmetology profession licensees, at least two of whom are cosmetologists.
  • One cosmetology school license holder or operator.
  • One licensed body art practitioner and one tanning facility owner and licensed operator.
  • Two members representing the general public.

Source: K.S.A. 74-2701
